&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;For this project, my team's goal was to deliver a formal, collaborative research proposal designed to identify a real-world problem and create an actual, credible study to solve it.
As a team of four, we practiced technical writing skills such as being persuasive and evidence-based while also pitching something realistic and presentable. 
We decided to tackle the shuttle service at UCF and present evidence as to how the shuttles could better meet and prioritize student needs.
Our target audience was the UCF Parking and Transportation Services leaders.

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;To come up with the idea, we brainstormed some student frustrations that were able to be examined and studied.
At this time, I was expressing my frustrations with the UCF shuttle service, and noticing that others also harbored similar sentiments, we chose that as the topic that we would advocate for change in. 
In particular, we wanted to highlight the schedule gaps of the shuttles. 
The shuttles would only run until 10 p.m. on weekdays, while the UCF library was open until 1 a.m., and the shuttles did not operate on weekends, while the library stayed open. 


&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;Our plan to get concrete data was to create a survey on Google Forms that we would distribute via QR code at shuttle stops and inside the shuttles themselves.
Along with this form, we would conduct follow-up interviews and conduct physical counts of shuttle use to get more data.
To encourage engagement, we would randomly choose 30 students who filled out a survey to get a 10 dollar gift card, bringing our budget for this study to $300 for the gift cards, and around $24 for all the physical flyers we would print out. 

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;As for revisions, I explicitly stated who would be surveyed, defined the qualifications for receiving a gift card, improved grammar and structure, as well as added another source to our background section.
I explicitly stated that our study population would be UCF students who take the shuttles, instead of leaving it assumed. I fixed grammar mistakes and clarified some points we tried to make, as well as added another source strengthening our argument that UCF needed to better prioritize the needs of students with the shuttles. 

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;Overall, this document exposed me to true professional group work, where we held each other accountable and divided work equally among everyone. 
It also helped me take into consideration all the things that a technical communicator needs to when making a document for work, such as strict word count limits and deadlines. 
Other than that, it helped me make a cohesive, whole document that can be used persuasively to incite meaningful, evidence-backed change.
