&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;The purpose of this project was to create a tailored resume and cover letter for a real-world internship.
This was meant to get me to think more about audience and about highlighting information that is beneficial for presenting my qualifications to recruiters and hiring managers.
For this project, my chosen internship was one for IDX Exchange as a Software Developer Intern. 

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;I structured my resume to highlight my status as a student first and foremost, then to state my qualifications and technical readiness for this position. 
Specifically, I chose projects that highlighted the skills that IDX Exchange demanded from their software developer interns such as experience developing a full-stack web application and working with REST APIs.

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;For revisions, I updated my street address, aligned my sections better, and listed my skills in order of proficiency.
I left-aligned my skills section, which was the major offender of being out of format, and instead of including the proficiency level of my skills separately, in order to be more visually appealing and more concise, I ordered my skills in order of proficiency. 
This solves the problem of me not liking having more words next to my skills and letting the hiring manager know which skills I am more proficient in and letting my projects do the main heavy-lifting when it comes to showcasing my proficiency.

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;Overall, even though I had made a resume before this, this project made me really scrutinize the formatting and design choices that go into making a resume and putting a section where it is. 
I learned that education is the most important thing that hiring managers want to see when applying to an intern-level position and that everything is better left-aligned, since we humans are left-to-right readers.
