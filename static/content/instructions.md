&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;For this project, my task was to create a set of written instructions accompanied by visuals for a topic of my choice. 
The objective for this document was to learn how to divide a complicated task into smaller, more digestible tasks and to use clear language and meaningful visuals to present a series of steps to a reader.
The document also had to be 800-1,000 words long. This was strictly enforced to make us aware of the highly specific guidelines that technical writers must follow. 
For this assignment, I chose to instruct a reader on how to create their first GitHub repository, since GitHub is such a widely used tool in the software development environment.

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;In terms of my formatting choices, I began my document with the "why," informing a reader about why they would want to know how to create a repository rather than jumping into how they should create one.
Along with this, to present this information in the best way for the reader, I split the larger ideas and tasks into more manageable chunks, guiding the reader from making a GitHub account to making their own repository through different heading levels and clear wording.
To round off these choices, I designed my visuals to actively guide the reader through the document, utilizing color and labels in annotated images to best accomplish this. 

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;For revisions, I specifically added an introductory paragraph in order to outline who my target audience was and the purpose of my document as a whole. 
Moreover, I cleared up the organization and visual hierarchy, making major ideas into level-one headings and demoting supporting tasks into lower-level headings. 
To sum up my revisions, I moved all annotated images to after the steps that described them. This cleared up confusion about which visual matched with specific instruction steps. 

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;All in all, creating this instructions document helped me consider the relationship between images and text, understand when readers need explicit direction for something, and connect real-world technical communication with a document. 
Having to see a topic that I know a lot about through a beginner's lens helped me plan where users might need some extra guidance and direction. 
The 800-1,000 word limit also forced me to consider what sections were needed without sacrificing clarity and helped me see how annotated visuals can show readers what to do without taking up word count.
